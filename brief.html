Project brief · for review

Landmark Globe

An interactive spinning Earth made for a curious 4-year-old — tap a landmark, watch the globe fly there, and discover fun facts with big pictures, gentle motion, and delight.

Awaiting your feedback before build

1 Vision

A single, self-contained web page that feels like a toy atlas: a glowing 3D globe in the centre of the screen, dotted with colourful landmark markers. When she taps one, the Earth turns toward it, a friendly card pops open, and she hears / sees a short story about that place.

Designed for little fingers, short attention spans, and big wonder — not for dense geography lessons.

Ages ~3–6 Touch-first Works offline once loaded One HTML file + assets

2 How it will be built

One main page (index.html) with embedded CSS and JavaScript, plus a small data file for landmarks. No app install, no login — open in a browser (Safari / Chrome) on iPad, laptop, or phone.

Technical approach

  • 3D globe via a lightweight library (Globe.gl / Three.js from CDN) so the Earth looks real and can spin, zoom, and fly to places.
  • Earth texture with bright oceans, continents, and a soft night-side glow for atmosphere.
  • Landmark data as a simple list: name, country, lat/lng, emoji/icon, short kid fact, fun “wow” detail, and optional sound cue.
  • No backend — everything runs in the browser.
  • Large tap targets (markers ~44px+) so a 4-year-old can hit them easily.
Parent tip: You’ll open the file (or a tiny local server) and leave it full-screen. She drags the globe, taps pins, closes cards — that’s the whole loop.

3 What she sees on screen

Title bar

Friendly name like “World Adventures!” with a soft sun / star motif — not a busy dashboard.

The globe (hero)

Full centre of the page. Slowly auto-rotates until she touches it, then she takes over.

Landmark pins

Glowing coloured dots + tiny emoji icons (🗼 🗽 🏰) that gently bounce so they’re easy to spot.

Fact card

Big illustration, landmark name, 2–3 short sentences in simple words, a “fun fact”, and a close button.

Explore strip

Optional bottom row of landmark thumbnails she can tap if spinning the globe is hard.

Sky / space backdrop

Soft gradient sky → space with twinkling stars behind the globe so it feels magical, not clinical.

4 Landmarks (starter set ~18–22)

A worldwide mix she’ll recognise from books, cartoons, or trips — each with a kid-level story.

Europe

Eiffel Tower · Big Ben · Colosseum · Leaning Tower of Pisa · Brandenburg Gate · Acropolis

Americas

Statue of Liberty · Golden Gate Bridge · Christ the Redeemer · Machu Picchu · CN Tower

Asia & Oceania

Great Wall · Taj Mahal · Sydney Opera House · Mount Fuji · Petronas Towers

Africa & Middle East

Pyramids of Giza · Sphinx · Table Mountain · Burj Khalifa

What each landmark card includes

  • Big friendly name — e.g. “The Eiffel Tower”
  • Where it is — “Paris, France” in large type
  • Picture / illustration — clear photo or emoji-led art (age-safe, no scary ruins vibe)
  • Short story (2–3 sentences) — what it is, in words a 4-year-old gets
  • Wow fact — one delightful detail (“It’s as tall as ~80 giraffes stacked!”)
  • Colour accent matching the pin so the UI feels cohesive
Easy to extend: Adding more landmarks later is just adding entries to the data list — no redesign needed.

5 Interactions (how she plays)

  1. Drag / swipe the globe to spin the Earth and hunt for pins.
  2. Pinch / scroll to zoom a little closer (with gentle limits so she can’t get lost in space).
  3. Tap a pin → globe smoothly flies & rotates to centre that landmark.
  4. Fact card slides up with bounce; pin pulses while selected.
  5. Tap “Next adventure” (optional) to jump to a random other landmark — surprise mode.
  6. Tap a thumbnail in the explore strip as a shortcut if globe spinning is tricky.
  7. Close the card → globe resumes soft auto-spin.
  8. Keyboard / parent assist — arrow keys or a “help me spin” button if needed.

6 Animations — detailed plan

Motion is the magic. Everything should feel soft, bouncy, and rewarding — never sudden or scary.

Globe motion

  • Idle auto-rotate — slow, constant spin (~1 full turn / 40–60s) so the world feels alive while she’s watching.
  • Touch takeover — auto-rotate pauses the moment a finger/mouse touches; resumes a few seconds after release.
  • Fly-to animation — when a landmark is chosen, camera arcs toward it over ~1.2–1.8s with easing (ease-in-out), not a hard cut.
  • Settle wobble — tiny overshoot when the fly-to finishes so it feels playful, not robotic.
  • Zoom breathing — optional very subtle in/out pulse while idle (almost imperceptible) to give “alive” presence.

Landmark pins

  • Entrance stagger — pins pop onto the globe one-by-one when the page loads (cascade over ~1.5s).
  • Idle bounce — each pin gently floats up/down on a slightly different timing so the globe sparkles with life.
  • Glow pulse — soft radial glow expands/contracts behind each pin.
  • Hover / focus grow — pin scales up ~1.3× with a brighter glow when pointed at.
  • Selected state — chosen pin does a happy “pop” then a continuous ring ripple while the card is open.
  • Sparkle burst — short particle sparkles at the pin when tapped (confetti-lite, 8–12 dots, fade out).

Fact card

  • Slide + spring up from the bottom (or side on desktop) with a soft bounce.
  • Backdrop dim — rest of the scene gently darkens so focus stays on the card.
  • Illustration reveal — image fades/scales in slightly after the card arrives.
  • Text cascade — title → place → story → wow fact appear in a short staggered fade (keeps reading paced).
  • Close animation — card drops away; dim lifts; pin ripple stops.

Page / atmosphere

  • Star twinkle — background stars randomly brighten/dim.
  • Shooting star — rare (every ~20–40s) soft streak across the sky for delight.
  • Title shimmer — subtle highlight sweep on the page title every so often.
  • Button press feedback — scale down on press, spring back on release (satisfying for little fingers).
  • Random adventure — button spins a tiny dice/globe icon before jumping.
  • First-visit intro — short “Tap the glowing dots!” coach mark that fades after first tap (doesn’t nag).

Reduced motion

  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion: cut idle bounce, sparkles, and shooting stars; keep simple fades and fly-to only.

7 Visual design — detailed plan

Look & feel

  • Direction: “storybook space atlas” — warm, bright, magical; not dark sci-fi or purple neon.
  • Palette: deep ocean blue globe, creamy UI surfaces, coral / sunshine / teal accents for pins and buttons.
  • Typography: rounded, friendly display font for titles; very readable rounded sans for body (large type, high contrast).
  • No clutter: first view = title + globe + pins. Cards and lists only appear when needed.

Globe visuals

  • Colourful Earth texture (greens, blues, soft deserts) — easy to recognise continents.
  • Subtle atmosphere haze / blue rim light around the sphere.
  • Soft shadow under the globe so it “sits” in space.
  • Night lights optional (city sparkles) — can be toggled off if too busy for her.
  • Clouds layer (semi-transparent) slowly drifting for depth.

Pins & markers

  • Colour-coded by continent or by “theme” (buildings / nature / statues).
  • Emoji or simple icon above a teardrop / circle marker.
  • White outline so pins stay visible over bright land or dark ocean.
  • Selected pin gets a gold/sun ring.

Fact cards

  • Large rounded panel, chunky close (X) button in the corner.
  • Hero image area with soft gradient matching the landmark colour.
  • Wow fact in a sun-coloured callout bubble (the “favourite bit”).
  • Optional “hear it” speaker button if we add read-aloud later.

Responsive / devices

  • iPad / tablet landscape — primary target; globe fills most of the screen.
  • Phone — globe still central; card becomes full-width bottom sheet.
  • Desktop — same experience with mouse drag; explore strip easier to use.

8 Sound (optional layer)

Kept gentle and opt-in so it never startles.

  • Soft “pop” when a pin is tapped.
  • Whoosh during fly-to (very short, airy).
  • Chime when the fact card opens.
  • Mute toggle always visible (starts muted by default for first visit).
  • Later stretch: simple read-aloud of the fact using browser speech synthesis.

9 Kid-safety & parent-friendly choices

  • No ads, no tracking, no external accounts.
  • No links out to the open web from landmark cards (keeps her in the experience).
  • Language is warm and simple — no scary history (wars, disasters).
  • Images curated / illustrated to be bright and friendly.
  • Works full-screen; explore strip helps if fine motor control for dragging is still developing.

12 Out of scope (for v1)

  • Quizzes, scores, or accounts
  • AR / camera modes
  • Multiplayer or sharing
  • Dense Wikipedia-style articles
  • Every landmark on Earth (curated set only)

13 Success looks like

  • She can find and open a landmark within seconds, unassisted.
  • The fly-to + card moment makes her want “another one!”
  • You can leave her on it for a stretch without worry (safe, contained, calm).
  • Adding her newest favourite landmark later is a 2-minute edit.
